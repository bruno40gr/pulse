# Odeon AI vision

Agreed direction: November 15, 2026. This is a product direction and proposed
sequence, not a claim that live agent operations already exist or are approved.

## North star

Humans own relationships, judgment and exceptions. Agents coordinate routine
work. One trustworthy academy operating system serves both.

Do not optimize for a human spending all day navigating every workspace. Do not
replace those workspaces with a chatbot over uncertain data either. Natural
language expresses intent; structured interfaces explain consequences, expose
business state and give people meaningful control.

## Next big rock

After completing the Beta integration safely, prove one authoritative operating
workflow end to end. Rescheduling is the recommended first slice, subject to
checking whether subscription administration is a larger real staff burden.

Build the foundation through that workflow, not an open-ended architecture
rewrite:

- Authoritative ownership of families, students, subscriptions, lessons,
  attendance, teacher/room availability and payments. Sample/browser state is
  not a live system of record.
- Explicit notice, credit, cancellation, capacity, eligibility and approval rules.
- Validated business actions that enforce permissions and policy. Agents do not
  improvise database edits or bypass the same checks used by humans.
- Conflict detection, duplicate-action protection, durable history and recovery.
- Deliberate data access and communication consent, including family/student
  isolation. Live impersonation needs permissions, audit and a mutation policy.

The model interprets and coordinates; application services enforce what is
allowed. Use deterministic automation for fixed rules and reminders. Use agents
where conversation, ambiguity or coordination justify them.

## First proposed workflow: scheduling concierge

Start in shadow/proposal mode, then staff approval, then narrowly bounded
automatic execution only after measured evidence supports it.

1. Identify the authorized family and affected lesson or recurring arrangement.
2. Clarify one-off versus ongoing changes.
3. Check teacher, room, service, capacity, notice and credit constraints.
4. Propose genuinely valid options and explain consequences.
5. Recheck availability before committing an approved action.
6. Apply the change without duplicate bookings, credits or notifications.
7. Notify the appropriate people and retain an attributable action history.
8. Escalate ambiguity, policy exceptions and cases with no valid option.

Success means the correct lesson moved, records agree, no resource was
double-booked and the family understood the result—not merely a fluent answer.

## Human/agent responsibility boundaries

| Area | Proposed agent role | Human ownership |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subscriptions | Explain options, prepare changes, coordinate approved reminders | Disputes, unusual refunds, exceptions and policy |
| Marketing | Draft campaigns, suggest audiences, prepare follow-ups and summarize results | Positioning, consent policy, budget and approval |
| Engagement | Surface missed lessons, unanswered requests and incomplete onboarding; draft outreach | Sensitive conversations and relationship repair |
| Churn | Present observable warning signs and suggested interventions | Interpret the situation and make personal contact |
| Operations | Triage requests, gather missing information and coordinate routine tasks | Staffing, safeguarding and exceptions |
| Rescheduling | Gather constraints, find valid options and execute permitted moves | Overrides and ambiguous/high-impact cases |

Start churn work with explainable observations rather than unsupported prediction
scores. Financial actions, external messaging and sensitive decisions need
explicit limits; autonomy is earned per action/case type, not granted globally.

## Human experience

Evolve toward an operations inbox plus business visibility:

- Needs attention: exceptions, unresolved requests and relationship concerns.
- Ready for approval: proposed actions, checked policies and consequences.
- Completed automatically: attributable actions with outcomes and recovery paths.

Each item opens the real calendar/family/subscription context. Keep those
interfaces as inspection and exception-handling tools. Avoid creating a new
full-time job approving every trivial action.

## Delivery sequence and evidence

1. Finish the current integration and its actual release gates.
2. Select and make one live workflow authoritative end to end.
3. Introduce agent proposals and compare them with staff decisions.
4. Measure staff time per request, successful resolution, corrections,
   escalations, policy violations and family experience.
5. Expand autonomy by proven case type; then extend into subscriptions and
   engagement. Maintain repeatable evaluations and explicit stop/escalation paths.

No schedule, staffing replacement percentage or unattended-operation promise is
implied. Current sample-only workflows are not evidence of live readiness.

## Anti-over-polishing agreement

Call out over-polishing explicitly before proceeding when work is mostly a
cosmetic variation, repeated spacing/copy churn, another parallel component, or a
new screen without a demonstrated task need. Say what higher-value work it would
displace and recommend deferring it. The user retains the decision.

Keep investing where there is a concrete correctness, security, accessibility,
comprehension, consistency or task-completion benefit. Release regression fixes
are not cosmetic polish. Small design changes can be worthwhile when they remove
real friction; do not use this rule to dismiss usability problems.

Judge progress by work eliminated and operational trust gained, not screen count.
